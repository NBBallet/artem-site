Concept

From the first fire to resurrection

A cosmogony of the fire that lives within the human being.

This production begins with the first cause of a mysterious fire. With the fire that came before the birth of humankind — or, more precisely, before the birth of human self-awareness.

Stravinsky wrote a score about victory over darkness. We ask: what is darkness today? How much do we truly know about our own existence today? How deep have we dared to dive into the rabbit hole?

A ballet in which the audience recognizes their own story and leaves with the feeling that something mysterious is awakening within.

Key Characters
The Mother

The eternal first cause. She exists before the beginning of the world and after its end. The fire that is always creating and uniting in love.

The Firebird

A living force born of the Great Mother. She pours light into common clay, and a human is born. She flies into the darkness to create and to awaken that which sleeps.

The Prince

A man born from the inspiration of the mystical Bird. Yet he is human and, of course, chooses the fruit from the tree… Our reading of the Prince is an archetype familiar to everyone.

Koschei's Kingdom

A living system of illusions where the rules are set by the shadow. A city that has forgotten itself.

“Culture is the veneration of Light. Culture is love for the human being. Where the hand and brain grow weary, the heart is invincible — and the heart is the Power of Light, the heart is the core of Culture.”
Nicholas Roerich
Visual Universequestion ①

Three key spatial and conceptual scenes

N. Roerich, Mother of the World, 1924
I

The Mother's Space

Boundless dark blue. The entire horizon is depth. A figure emerges from light, somewhere high upon a mountainous canvas.

El Lissitzky, Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge, 1920
II

Koschei's Kingdom

A geometric labyrinth. A matrix. Constructivist trusses and beams in space. Instead of the Middle Ages — a space-time utopia. People move as in ordinary life, but there is something of the machine in them.

N. Roerich, Himalayas, Sikkim, late 1920s
III

Resurrection

The geometry of Koschei's kingdom falls apart. The fire becomes living and embodied. People — women and men — become organically alive, casting off their attachments to the machine. Roerich's space: gold, ultramarine, a fiery horizon.

Costumes

Mother

White. The silhouette of an icon in Roerich's style. A fabric that breathes. No adornments — only form and light.

Firebird

Structured, geometric. Fire crystals instead of feathers. Between human and superhuman.

Prince

Earthly, natural tones. He is the contrast and the point of recognition for the viewer.

Koschei's Agents

Graphic black and grey. Constructivist accents. All identical — this is their key feature.

The Sleeping City

Linen-like, organic. They are like a backdrop that gradually comes to life and discovers its own colour.
