## Abstract (ICARE)

ICARE is an evening in two parts. The first is Igor Markevitch's L'Envol d'Icare (1932), a score
written for Serge Lifar and never staged as a ballet. Lifar could not bring his rhythm to it.
I come back to it. Part one holds the frame of the research. Part two is born in the field, with
the creative team: composer, designer, dramaturg, dancers. My question: can the hard vertical of
a production carry the horizontal of co-creation? Eight months, five to seven dancers.

## Project Description (ICARE)

A Greek myth and its embodiment today. How should a myth be read? Where is the body in it? What
can choreography do in theatre that no other art can?

Igor Markevitch wrote L'Envol d'Icare in 1932 for Serge Lifar. Lifar refused: he could not bring
his rhythm into accord with the score. In 1935 he danced his own Icare without music, on a rhythm
entirely his own. The score has never been staged. Both were born in Kyiv, and Kyiv formed me too.

Icarus escapes the tyrant and refuses his father's instructions. But can he pass through the sun,
or become it, instead of falling? Occupied France, 1943: Matisse cut his Icare from paper, a red
heart in a black body like night. A sun inside the infinity of a human being.

War. The moment a person risks everything and feels fully alive. A wounded soldier who could stay
home and keeps going back to the front. A woman buying expensive cosmetics after a sleepless night
under shells. Filling in this form, I lost a fourth friend in a war Russia started. Can I speak as
an artist now, and about what? How does an artistic voice work when you lose your mother tongue,
country, home, space for art, familiar people?

I work with the composer's own version for two pianos and three percussion: seven movements,
27 minutes. Bartók heard it; it is believed to have shaped his Sonata for two pianos and percussion.

For nineteen years I worked inside the hardest vertical there is: live broadcasts, state ceremonies,
arenas of ten thousand. Since 2013 I have practised the inverse: the phenomenology of the working
field. The field already knows what the group has to create. My work is to take that knowledge out
of the space. Not only dancers: composer, designer, dramaturg, the whole production enter the same
field. ICARE puts both strategies inside one evening. Part one holds the structure. Part two is born
in the field, in research. K3 is where the method can be extended and tested. Eight months is the
most honest horizon.

## Short biography (канонічна англійська версія)

Artem Hordieiev is a Ukrainian choreographer and director. He came into dance through sport:
gymnastics, folk dance, then ballroom, where he became bronze medallist of the World Cup 2005.

At 18 the television industry took him. Ukrainian prime time, live broadcasts, an audience of
millions. At the same time he trained in classical ballet every morning in Kyiv, to work in an
opera and ballet theatre. For ten years he lived in two bodies: one for the camera, one for the
ballet stage.

He danced in Denys Matvienko's company in Ukraine and Europe. He directed large entertainment
formats in Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Tajikistan and, until 2015, Russia. At 30, in 2018, he ended his
career as a dancer. He had directed and choreographed for years, but for television. He wanted the
theatre. In Ukraine, there was no institutional support for a choreographer to work there permanently.

His subject is the human spirit under pressure: freedom, identity and maturity tested by war, risk
of death, loss, different forms of dictatorship. He did not choose this subject. His practice was
formed inside revolution and war: Ukraine, Central Asia, Russia, Senegal.

His method comes from the phenomenology of the working field, adapted to dramaturgy and the stage.
He wrote his diploma thesis on it and has been developing it since.

In 2013, he founded LITSO Dance Company in Kyiv, today Newspaper Birds Ballet. He works with the
producer Tetiana Hordieieva. He is based in France and works across Europe.
